# 📦 PPT Composite Box Design

Lightweight composite box enclosure designed for waterjet manufacturing, structural performance, and manufacturable assembly.

---

## 📐 Overview

This project involved the design, manufacturing, and verification of a composite enclosure using CAD modeling, nested DXF manufacturing layouts, structural FEA analysis, waterjet cutting, and physical assembly.

### Key Objectives

- Lightweight structural design
- Waterjet manufacturability
- Tab-based assembly alignment
- Structural stiffness verification
- Composite manufacturing compatibility
- Practical assembly and fabrication

---

## 🛠️ Software Used

- Fusion 360
- ANSYS Mechanical
- CATIA V5

---

## 🧵 Composite Layup Structure

The enclosure was designed using a hybrid composite sandwich structure consisting of:

- Kevlar outer layer
- Carbon fiber layer
- 1/4 inch Divinycell core
- Carbon fiber layer
- Kevlar inner layer

This layup configuration was selected to balance:

- Impact resistance
- Structural stiffness
- Lightweight performance
- Energy absorption
- Composite manufacturability
- Electrical insulation

## 🔧 Manufacturing & Assembly

Following the completion of the CAD and simulation stages, the enclosure was physically manufactured and assembled from the composite panels.

### Waterjet-Cut Composite Faces

![Waterjet Cut Face](faceCutout.JPEG)

The individual composite enclosure faces were produced from the finalized flat-pattern geometry. The puzzle-fit tab design helped maintain alignment between panels while converting the original CAD assembly into manufacturable components.

### Epoxy Assembly

![Epoxying Box](epoxyingbox.JPEG)

The enclosure faces were aligned and bonded using epoxy to form the final structure. This stage provided an opportunity to evaluate the practical fit of the tab geometry, manufacturing tolerances, and overall assembly process.

### Completed Composite Box

![Completed Composite Box](CompleteBox.JPEG)

The completed enclosure demonstrated the full design-to-manufacturing workflow, progressing from CAD geometry and structural analysis to waterjet-cut composite panels and final bonded assembly.

## ✅ Project Conclusion

The completed PPT composite box successfully progressed from an initial enclosure concept to a physically manufactured assembly. CAD modeling was used to develop the enclosure geometry and tab system, while ANSYS FEA provided structural verification prior to fabrication. The individual composite panels were then prepared as nested DXF files, waterjet cut, and epoxy bonded into the final enclosure.

Manufacturing the design also provided practical validation of the panel geometry, tab alignment, tolerances, and assembly method. The project demonstrated an end-to-end engineering workflow combining composite material selection, mechanical design, simulation, design for manufacturing, and hands-on fabrication.
